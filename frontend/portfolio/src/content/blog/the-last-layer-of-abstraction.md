Abstraction is in my view the concept that allowed the human race to evolve the most. Without it we would always be **reinventing the wheel** in the most literal sense, for each generation. Abstractions serve as the checkpoint for human knowledge, specially in complex systems.

In the field of software engineering, such abstractions have allowed us to move from *fiddling with bits of information* to being able to download a library that will have us playing Minecraft in under 10 LOC.

This process has always been natural, and jumping from a paradigm to another always felt like the right direction in terms of what allowed turning something complex into something simple, reproducible and understandable for bigger amounts of people.

And then came the monster. In the attempt to not mention it, AI has once again found its way into my article and made me wonder about its role in this never ending cycle of abstraction. From the way I see it, there are two ways the game could be played with AI:

1. AI becomes the final step in abstraction because everything can be understood and shared via natural language
2. AI becomes the keeper of abstraction, continues the infinite cycle of it alone and we are left out. Instead of abstraction hiding complexity, it starts hiding reasoning

But what happens then? How can knowledge travel between minds if they are filled with shortcuts and assumptions? Does it even need to be transferred?

I like to believe that our curiosity is endless, and that knowledge finds a way, but the assumptions will grow bigger, and the steps will become harder to take in the right direction. Newer engineers will be capable of producing the **exact same** result as more senior peers, but what are the consequences of it? ***Did we actually need to learn about bits?***

Unfortunately there is no way of knowing, as we are in the middle of the change. We are building the systems that empower the abstraction break, understanding where it might take us and regulating it all at the same time. This is the unfortunate consequence of dealing with palpable change in information handling while making it available to the masses.
